\textbf{Purpose and positioning.}
The manuscript evaluates recovery of publicly recorded human gamma/delta TCR
partners from one CDR3. Its contribution is a controlled benchmark separating
sequence priors, conditional information, free candidate availability,
ranking and exposure. BMC Bioinformatics is the primary preparation target;
the accompanying journal comparison assesses two alternatives.

\textbf{Central finding.}
Across the primary component-disjoint splits, conditional NLL is 1.005 nats
per target token, compared with 1.013 for the matched prior and 1.115 for
random initialization. However, primary likelihood Hit@5 at 64 draws is only
0.03\%. Separate ranking does not show a reproducible primary improvement.
Higher recovery in exposed-target populations coexists with substantial
prior performance and concentrated events. Recorded recovery is not evidence
of receptor function.

\textbf{Reading order.}
Start with the structured abstract, Figure 1, and Results 3.1--3.3 with
Tables 1--3. Figures 4--6 then distinguish constructed retrieval from free
generation and explain graph-composition/exposure sensitivity.
Discussion 4.1--4.4 states the interpretation and limits.
Additional file 1 provides full methods, sensitivity analyses and Figures
S1--S3; Additional files 2--6 retain numerical and reproducibility evidence.

\textbf{Questions for the supervisor.}
\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
\item Is the benchmark question and contribution sufficiently clear for
BMC Bioinformatics, and is the proposed title appropriate?
\item Are the controls, uncertainty units and interpretation of sparse hits
convincing, especially in relation to target priors and sequence neighborhoods?
\item Are the source concentration, distant-pair scarcity and lack of
donor-independent validation adequately bounded?
\item Is further evidence needed before submission, or should the current
bounded benchmark be submitted first? Would a higher-positioned journal
justify a separate study extension?
\end{enumerate}
